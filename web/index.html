MSMARCO passages are already human-curated retrieval units, so splitting them destroys information. On Gujarati, parent_child collapses to 0.389 against passage_native's 0.626. Semantic chunking cost 719s of build time to finish fourth — 400× the winner's 1.8s for 5 points less recall.

MSMARCO queries are natural language, which e5 already handles, and rank_bm25 does no stemming for Devanagari or Gujarati — so its candidates displace better dense hits during fusion. The sparse index is still built and wired in behind a weight of 0.

03 — Embedded Qdrant scans single-threaded

Tripling the corpus put a quarter of requests over budget, and doubling vCPUs changed nothing — the scan never leaves one thread. Per-stage profiling found retrieve at 126ms against 6.5ms locally, a 20× gap where every other stage scaled ~6×. Vectors are L2-normalised, so cosine is one M @ q matmul that numpy hands to multi-threaded BLAS.

changebeforeafter
exact search on a BLAS matmul126ms3.3ms
drop BM25 fusion11.6ms0ms
partition index by language70ms9.9ms
cascade the grounding checks130ms0.2ms
warm encoder + page in matrices at boot~450ms first call~2.4ms

Together these paid for a 3.3× larger corpus (60,464 → 202,201 chunks) while lowering worst-case latency, on fewer vCPUs. Verified equivalent: the numpy path returns identical top-10 to Qdrant on 59 of 60 queries, the one difference being a score tie. HNSW is right at millions of vectors; at 60k it would trade recall for a speedup exact search already has.

04 — Guardrail thresholds are calibrated, per language

Swept over 600 in-corpus and 52 deliberately out-of-corpus queries, choosing the point that maximises Youden's J under a 10% false-accept cap. multilingual-e5 scores are not comparable across scripts, so one global threshold over-refuses the weakest language:

en0.9070.853−0.0080.88486.0%10.0%
hi0.8970.859−0.0130.88072.6%6.2%
gu0.8720.857−0.0210.87729.8%6.2%

Stated limit: separation is negative for Gujarati. Even with its own operating point at a 6.2% false-accept rate it accepts only 29.8% of answerable Gujarati questions, and buying that back to 76.2% costs 25% false accepts. That is a property of multilingual-e5-small on a lower-resource language, and no threshold fixes it — a stronger encoder would. Grounding uses token overlap (grounded 0.778 vs ungrounded 0.010, admitting 0.7%) and escalates to the encoder only when overlap says no.

Cold and warm are both reported; quoting only the warm number is the standard way to make a RAG pipeline look faster than it is. The LLM call itself (P50 309ms, P100 706ms) and Sarvam STT are third-party network hops, measured and reported separately — never folded into the core figure. A round trip from India to Groq is ~250ms before the model does anything, so no configuration puts a generative answer inside 200ms.
